Market Fear vs Market Performance — VIX & Nasdaq

Two daily-closing charts · FRED / Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis

VIX — expected US market volatility
Nasdaq Composite — US technology-heavy shares

Did you know? The VIX reflects expected 30-day volatility in the S&P 500, not the Nasdaq itself. A sharp VIX rise alongside a Nasdaq decline often indicates increasing investor anxiety, but neither alone is a reliable sell signal.

These charts show separately scaled daily closing observations, not intraday live quotes. Source: VIXCLS and NASDAQCOM. If an image is blocked, click its chart area to open the FRED source.

Household credit — official quarterly update

Watch / mild deterioration. In Q3 2026, 5.0% of outstanding US household debt was delinquent, up from 4.7% in Q2. Early arrears increased for auto loans and mortgages, while credit-card early and serious delinquency measures improved slightly.

In simple terms: More households are falling behind overall, but stress is not worsening across every type of borrowing. This supports caution, not an automatic sell decision.
